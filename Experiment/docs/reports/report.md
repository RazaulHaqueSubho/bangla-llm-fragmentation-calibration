# Results Report: Tokenization Fragmentation and Calibration in Bangla LLMs

Thesis: *Does Tokenization Fragmentation Predict Miscalibration in Bangla LLMs? A Cross-Lingual Study.* All findings are observational associations.

Source: `Analysis_Report_6models_plus_phi_pluss_e4.pdf` (generated by `analysis_report_e4.py`), per-item CSVs, `tokenizer_fragmentation_per_item_all7.csv`, `tokenizer_fragmentation_per_item_all11.csv`, `dev_screen_report_e3.csv`.

## 1. Executive summary

1. Six main models (`titullm_1b`, `tigerllm_1b`, `llama32_3b`, `qwen25_3b`, `bloomz_3b`, `llama32_3b_base`) were evaluated on 1,500 BN and 1,500 EN items with cyclic4 debiasing. All 12 model-language cells are significantly above 25% chance.
2. Cyclic4 removed the raw letter collapse: raw top-letter share reached 0.784 (`titullm_1b`, BN), while cyclic4 shares sit between 0.268 and 0.313.
3. BN fragmentation is high for the Llama, Qwen and Phi tokenizers (TPW about 6.2-7.1) and low for Gemma and BLOOM (about 2.2-2.3). EN TPW is 1.6-2.0 for all models.
4. Higher TPW is associated with lower confidence and correctness, and with larger squared-Brier error, most clearly in English. ECE does not rise monotonically with TPW.
5. Model-level calibration differs more than fragmentation level does. `qwen25_3b` is strongly overconfident (ECE 0.273 BN, 0.206 EN); `tigerllm_1b` is best calibrated.
6. `phi35_mini` is exploratory. Of four E4 models, three failed dev gates and `sarvam1_2b` failed the option-mass rule, so none enters a calibration table.

### 3.1 Raw versus cyclic4

Raw top-letter shares: `titullm_1b` BN 0.784 (B) and EN 0.735 (B); `tigerllm_1b` BN 0.473 (C); `bloomz_3b` EN 0.543 (C). ECE shifts from raw to cyclic4: `qwen25_3b` BN 0.430 to 0.273; `tigerllm_1b` BN 0.166 to 0.023; `llama32_3b` BN 0.090 to 0.024; `llama32_3b_base` BN 0.019 to 0.062 (rises). Cyclic4 changes what confidence means, so raw and cyclic4 values are not directly comparable.

## 6. Fragmentation versus calibration

- **TPW quartiles:** ECE is not monotone in TPW (for example `llama32_3b` BN Q1-Q4: 0.017, 0.045, 0.067, 0.037; `qwen25_3b` EN: 0.233, 0.151, 0.262, 0.203). Quartile CIs overlap heavily.
- **Logistic (correctness ~ TPW + word count):** coefficient negative in all 12 cells; nominally significant in 7.
- **Linear (confidence ~ TPW + word count):** negative in 11 of 12 cells (`titullm_1b` BN slightly positive); nominally significant in 9; largest in EN (`llama32_3b` -0.187, `qwen25_3b` -0.137, `tigerllm_1b` -0.115).
- **Spearman (TPW vs confidence):** mostly negative in EN (-0.04 to -0.32); mixed in BN (`titullm_1b` +0.18, `tigerllm_1b` -0.19).
- **Squared-Brier error term:** positive TPW coefficient in all 12 cells, nominally significant in 11. This outcome mixes accuracy and confidence.
- **Holm:** across 24 item-level TPW tests (12 logistic, 12 linear), 13 are significant at 0.05.

Rules R1-R7 and `e4_added_models_plan.json` were fixed before results were opened. All four E4 models are base models, so comparisons with the mostly instruction-tuned main set are confounded. The gate-failed models combine high BN TPW (4.83-10.05) with near-chance BN accuracy, so excluding them can bias any TPW-calibration relationship.

## 10. Limitations

Few tokenizer levels; tokenizer family, size, architecture and tuning vary together; translated English side; train-split evaluation; cyclic4 alters confidence semantics; small dev samples; one prompt format and one seed; empty dtype-sensitivity table; few subject clusters; observational design only.

## 11. Conclusion

With cyclic4 debiasing, six models yield usable BN and EN calibration data. Higher TPW is associated with lower confidence and correctness, most clearly in English, but ECE by TPW quartile is not monotone and model-level differences in calibration (especially `qwen25_3b` overconfidence) are larger than the fragmentation associations. With four tokenizer levels and heavy confounding, the evidence is descriptive and does not show that fragmentation drives miscalibration.
